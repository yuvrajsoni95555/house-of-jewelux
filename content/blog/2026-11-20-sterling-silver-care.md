Sterling silver is one of the most rewarding metals to own. It has a soft, cool shine that suits everyday wear and festive dressing alike, and it ages gracefully when it is looked after. The good news is that caring for silver does not need special equipment. A few minutes of attention and a handful of good habits are enough to keep most pieces looking the way they did on the day you first wore them.

## Why silver changes colour

Sterling silver is an alloy: 92.5 per cent pure silver mixed with a small amount of another metal, usually copper, to make it strong enough to wear. Silver reacts slowly with sulphur compounds in the air, and the result is a thin, dark layer called tarnish. It is not damage and it is not dirt in the usual sense; it is a surface reaction, and it can be removed.

Tarnish appears faster in some conditions than others:

- humid weather and coastal air
- contact with perfume, hair spray, lotions and sunscreen
- rubber bands, some paper and cardboard, and wool, which can release sulphur
- long periods in an open dish on a dressing table

> Silver that is worn often usually tarnishes less than silver that is stored away. The gentle friction of daily wear keeps the surface clean.

## Everyday habits that make the biggest difference

### Put it on last, take it off first

Make jewellery the final step when you get ready, after perfume, make-up and hair products have dried. In the evening, take it off before you wash your face or apply creams. This single habit prevents most build-up.

### Keep it out of water and chemicals

Remove silver before swimming, bathing, cleaning the house or working out. Chlorine in pools and household cleaners can mark the surface, and sweat contains salts that speed up tarnish.

### Wipe after wearing

A soft, lint-free cloth is your best tool. A quick wipe after each wear removes skin oils and moisture before they settle. A dedicated silver polishing cloth works well for regular touch-ups, but use it gently and avoid rubbing over stones or plated areas.

## How to clean silver at home

For light tarnish, the mildest method is usually the best:

1. Fill a bowl with lukewarm water and add a few drops of mild, unscented dish soap.
2. Let the piece rest for a few minutes.
3. Clean gently with a very soft toothbrush, paying attention to links and settings.
4. Rinse in clean water and dry completely with a soft cloth.
5. Let it air-dry for a little longer before you put it away.

Avoid toothpaste and baking-soda pastes. They are abrasive and can leave fine scratches, especially on high-polish surfaces. Avoid dips and strong chemical cleaners unless you know the piece has no stones, pearls, enamel or plating, because these can be dulled or damaged.

### Pieces that need extra care

Some jewellery should not be soaked at all. Pearls, opals, emeralds and many treated or porous stones can be harmed by water, heat or chemicals. Pieces with oxidised (deliberately darkened) details also need care, because aggressive cleaning removes the contrast that makes the design stand out. For these, a dry, soft cloth is the safest option, and anything more is best left to a jeweller.

## Storing silver properly

Good storage slows tarnish more than anything else:

- Keep each piece in its own soft pouch or a lined compartment so pieces do not scratch each other.
- Store in a cool, dry place, away from bathrooms and windows.
- Add a small anti-tarnish strip or a sachet of silica gel to the box to absorb moisture.
- Fasten chains before storing them so they do not knot.

If you have a piece you wear only for festivals or weddings, check on it every few months and give it a gentle wipe even if you have not worn it.

## When to see a professional

Some situations are better handled at the bench: deep, long-standing tarnish, loose stones, bent prongs, worn clasps or a chain that keeps catching. A jeweller can polish, check settings and repair small issues before they turn into lost stones. Many people have their most-worn pieces checked once a year, which is a sensible habit for rings and bracelets in particular, since they take the most knocks.

## A short checklist

- Jewellery on last, off first.
- No water, chemicals or exercise while wearing it.
- Wipe after every wear.
- Mild soap and a soft brush for cleaning, nothing abrasive.
- Separate pouches, a dry place, an anti-tarnish strip.

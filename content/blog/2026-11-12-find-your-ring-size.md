A ring that fits well is one you forget you are wearing. Too tight and it pinches by the end of the day; too loose and it spins, slides off when your hands are cold, or catches on clothes. Getting the size right before you order saves time, avoids resizing and, for some designs, protects the ring itself. Here is how to measure accurately at home.

## Before you measure: timing matters

Fingers change size through the day. They are usually smallest in the morning and when you are cold, and largest in the evening, after exercise or in hot weather. For the most accurate result:

- measure at the end of the day, when your hands are warm
- avoid measuring right after a workout, a hot shower or a salty meal
- measure two or three times on different days if you can

> Measure the finger you will actually wear the ring on. Your dominant hand is often slightly larger, and the same finger can differ by half a size between hands.

## Method 1: measure a ring that already fits

This is the most reliable home method, because it uses a ring you already know is comfortable.

1. Choose a ring that fits the intended finger well, ideally with a thin, round band.
2. Place it on a ruler and measure the **inside diameter** in millimetres, from inner edge to inner edge.
3. Measure across the widest point, and measure twice.
4. Compare your number with the size chart in our [size guide](/#size-guide).

If the ring you are measuring has a very wide band, choose a size slightly larger for a new ring with a narrow band, and the other way round. Wide bands feel tighter than narrow ones of the same size.

## Method 2: the paper strip

If you do not have a ring to measure, a strip of paper works well. Thread or string can stretch, so paper is the better choice.

1. Cut a strip of paper about 1 cm wide and 10 cm long.
2. Wrap it around the base of your finger, snug but not tight.
3. Mark the point where the paper overlaps.
4. Lay it flat and measure from the end to the mark in millimetres. This is your finger's circumference.
5. Check that the strip can slide over your knuckle; if your knuckle is larger than the base of your finger, use a size between the two measurements.

### Common mistakes with the paper method

- Wrapping too tightly, which gives a size that will feel uncomfortable.
- Measuring a cold finger in an air-conditioned room.
- Forgetting the knuckle, so the ring will not go on.
- Reading the measurement from the wrong edge of the mark.

## Method 3: a ring sizer

A plastic or metal ring sizer, the adjustable kind or a set of sizing rings, gives the most consistent results at home. Try the size you think is right, wear it for a few minutes, and make sure it slides over the knuckle with a little resistance and does not spin freely.

## Buying a ring as a surprise

Measuring someone else's finger without them knowing is a challenge, but there are good options:

- Borrow a ring they already wear on the same finger, and measure the inside diameter as in Method 1. Put it back quickly.
- Press the ring into a bar of soap to make an impression, which a jeweller can measure.
- Ask a close friend or family member, who may know their size.
- If you are unsure, choose a slightly larger size. Making a ring smaller is generally simpler than making it larger.

## Designs that need extra care with sizing

Some rings are harder or impossible to resize after they are made:

- eternity and half-eternity bands set with stones all the way round
- rings with engraving, enamel or patterns along the full band
- some tension and open-band designs

For these, it is worth measuring twice and, if possible, using a proper sizer. If you are unsure, [ask our concierge](/#contact) before you order and we will help you choose.

## The comfortable-fit test

When you try a ring on, it should:

- slide over the knuckle with gentle pressure
- sit at the base of the finger without leaving a deep mark
- not spin all the way round when you shake your hand
- feel the same in the morning and the evening

A good fit makes a ring something you wear every day rather than on special occasions. A few minutes with a ruler or a paper strip is all it takes.

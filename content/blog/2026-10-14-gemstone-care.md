Gemstones are often thought of as indestructible, but each one has its own strengths and weaknesses. Some are hard but brittle, some are soft, and some are sensitive to heat, light or chemicals. Knowing what you own is the first step to keeping it beautiful. This guide covers the stones you are most likely to find in a jewellery box, along with the general habits that protect all of them.

## Hardness is not the whole story

Gem hardness is usually described on the Mohs scale, from 1 (very soft) to 10 (diamond). A higher number means the stone resists scratches better. But hardness does not measure toughness, which is resistance to chipping and breaking. A stone can be hard yet still crack if it is knocked at the wrong angle.

> Treat every gemstone as if it could chip, even the hardest ones. Most damage happens in a moment: a knock against a door frame, a drop onto a tiled floor.

## Stone by stone

### Rubies and sapphires

Rubies and sapphires are both varieties of the mineral corundum, with a hardness of 9. They are among the most durable coloured stones for everyday wear. Warm water with a little mild soap and a soft brush is usually safe for untreated stones. Many rubies and sapphires are treated to improve colour or clarity, and some treatments are sensitive to heat or chemicals, so ask what treatment your stone has had before using anything stronger than soap and water.

### Emeralds

Emeralds are hard (7.5 to 8) but they usually contain natural inclusions, which make them more fragile than their hardness suggests. Most emeralds are also treated with oils or resins to improve clarity. For these reasons:

- avoid hot water, steam and ultrasonic cleaners
- clean with a soft, damp cloth and dry immediately
- avoid sudden temperature changes
- take emerald rings off for any work with your hands

### Diamonds

Diamonds are the hardest natural material, but they attract grease, which dulls their sparkle quickly. Soaking in warm soapy water and brushing gently behind the stone restores brightness. Diamonds can still chip along certain directions, so protect them from hard knocks.

### Pearls

Pearls are organic and soft (around 2.5 to 4.5), and they react to acids, perfume, cosmetics and even sweat. They need the gentlest care of all:

1. Put pearls on last, after perfume and make-up.
2. Wipe them with a soft, slightly damp cloth after every wear.
3. Never soak them; water can weaken the silk thread.
4. Store them flat, never hanging, so the thread does not stretch.
5. Have strung pearls restrung every few years if worn often.

## Habits that protect every stone

Whatever the gemstone, the same everyday habits help:

- Remove jewellery before cleaning, gardening, swimming or the gym.
- Store pieces separately so harder stones cannot scratch softer ones.
- Keep stones out of long, direct sunlight and away from heat.
- Check settings regularly by gently feeling for movement.
- Know which treatments your stones have had; it changes how they should be cleaned.

## Mistakes that cause lasting damage

Most serious gemstone damage comes from a small number of mistakes:

- Using ultrasonic or steam cleaners on emeralds, pearls, opals or treated stones.
- Cleaning with bleach, ammonia or harsh household products.
- Storing everything together in one pouch.
- Wearing rings with large stones during sport or heavy work.
- Ignoring a loose stone until it falls out.

## When to visit a jeweller

A professional check every year or two is worthwhile, especially for rings and bracelets that are worn daily. A jeweller can tighten settings, clean safely, and spot early wear on clasps and prongs before a stone is lost. If you are unsure how to clean a particular piece, it is always safer to ask than to experiment.
